# Mix Tab cohesion and the single Playhead readout

Partially supersedes [ADR-0008](./0008-four-tab-shell-and-single-toggle-transport.md): the Transport stays a single **Play/Pause toggle** and the **Master Waveform** spine stays the timeline surface, but the Mix Tab's rail order and its two competing `mm:ss` readouts no longer apply. Recorded at porting start for the amendment "Mix Tab cohesion and the single Playhead readout" of `.scratch/mix-rail-tidy-up/spec.md` (tickets `06`–`10`); the look is agent-authored via the documented dead-OpenDesign fallback.

## Decision

**One Playhead, one clock, one readout, rails ordered the way the work is done.**

The **Playhead** is one value — canonical seconds of the **Source Mix**, invariant under **Time-Stretch** — and the spine, the four **Stem Strips** and the **Transport** readout show that same value at every instant. The **Stem Player** gains one outbound position channel, in the shape `onPlayheadChange(timeSec)`, fed by the engine's existing position subscription; the app wires the **Master Waveform** spine to it. The spine's canvas repaint is time-gated near fifteen draws per second (`PLAYHEAD_REPAINT_CEILING_MS = 66` ms) while the position value keeps flowing every frame, so the assistive readout is never a stale approximation.

The **Transport** row owns the Mix Tab's only visible `mm:ss` readout. The spine header keeps its label and its "Overview" chip while its own `mm:ss / mm:ss` group dies outright; the canvas tick graduations survive because they are the time axis, not a position.

The rails read top-down in the order the work is done, one order at every breakpoint: spine → **Hand Calibration** tap lane → **Tempo Detector** candidate-picker **Rail** → **Transport** row → four **Stem Strips** → **Click Track Rail** → **Time-Stretch & Pitch-Shift**. The timeline neighbourhood gathers every surface that carries a time axis; the mixer block becomes five lanes with the **Click Track** last; rate and key come last. The candidate picker stops living inside the **Click Track Rail**: it becomes its own **Rail** — `role="group"` with its own accessible name, its four chips keeping their accessible names, `aria-pressed` lock state, confidence line and honest-ranking copy — rendered as the **Stem Player** section's first rail. No rail changes owner, no new component, no new seam, no mobile-specific Mix block.

## Why these trades

**Why exactly one `mm:ss` survives:** the drift symptom and the redundancy were one screenshot showing two bugs at once. Five **Playhead** renderings existed and only four agreed; a second clock made the disagreement legible, which is also what made it grieve. Fixing the channel without deleting a readout leaves redundancy; deleting a readout without the channel leaves a spine that still lies — both belong in one increment, and the live-mirror test (press play, no mixer event, the spine's `aria-valuenow` walks off its start) keeps the first from regressing silently.

**Why the Transport holds the clock instead of the spine:** the spine is a scrub surface — its whole job is the gesture against the time axis, and it already carries that axis as canvas graduations. The clock belongs to the transport that acts on a position, not to the surface you drag. Keeping the spine's readout and merely syncing both was rejected: two readouts of one value is a redundancy, not a feature, and the pair is exactly what made the lie visible.

**Why the picker is its own Rail and not a guest inside the Click Track lane:** a **Rail** means identity plus its own controls; the **Click Track**'s chips are not the lane's controls — they re-lock the **Beat Grid**, which is shared machinery the whole stage changes. A **Rail** that owns nothing but an on/off and a volume hosting four buttons betrays its own shape: the rail-shape pins assert zero buttons inside the lane root, so the relocation and the lane's shape assertion are one change, not two. Rendering it as the section's first rail is what keeps the chips physically out of the lane root while every pinned testid — picker, confidence line, readout — keeps resolving.

## Considered options

- Keep both readouts and sync them (rejected: redundancy; the disagreement was the symptom, the pair is the cause).
- Move the clock to the spine and drop the Transport's (rejected: the Transport is where position is acted on; the spine is the gesture surface).
- Per-frame mixer bridge reuse (rejected: the mixer-and-**Session** bridge keeps its own meaning and save cadence; a live bridge driving auto-save would churn persistence).
- Picker chips stay in the lane / move to app-level composition (rejected in both directions: the first betrays the lane's zero-button shape, the second would move ownership out of the component that already owns the picker — this is a placement increment, not a re-architecture).
- A mobile-specific Mix block to re-order on small screens (rejected outright: one order at every breakpoint, per ADR-0009's lesson that a drifting pair reintroduces the drift it removed).

What survives of the original decision is the machinery and the honesty, unchanged: the picker still asks the app for a re-lock hint through `onChooseTempoCandidate`, the picker group's accessible name carries the honest ranking copy (calibrated by hand, a solid reading, or the ranked count), and every chip — the locked one included — shows its **own** confidence, so no chip claims one BPM as certain. The user's card wins that argument; the rejection of app-level composition keeps its reason, because the picker stays owned by the component that already owns it. The chip family itself is one rule for every reading, per the user's card and the mock's chip classes (`p-1.5 rounded border flex items-center justify-between`): span1 `{bpm} BPM` and span2 `{pct}% match`, so the mock's role-word vocabulary — `100% CONFIDENCE`, `64% Match`, `53% (2x Double)`, `34% Alternate` — is replaced by the uniform `{pct}% match` everywhere, including the locked chip, and the coral leader treatment dies with it. And the chips are CONTENT-SIZED: `justify-items-center` in the island grid plus a `w-fit` island stop a dead grid cell from stretching a chip across the card — the mock's own 1fr cells measure 340px, ours measure the text — so the user's "compact chips, no long empty gap" wins that argument too.

## Amendment (2026-11-06, transport-snapshot — the shell reads one Transport snapshot; mirror states banned)

The architecture review (report 2026-11-05, candidate 1) diagnosed why five renderings of the transport facts once disagreed: the engine-owned facts — play state, Playhead, Master volume, post-fader peaks — were mirrored a **second time in App** before reaching the screen (the `footerTransportPlaying`/`footerMasterVolume`/`footerPeakLevels`/`currentTime` mirror states plus a mutable ref the player wrote into mid-render — all four states and the ref are DELETED by this increment), so nothing structurally guaranteed the disc, fader, % readout, Peak Pips, spine and clock chip showed the same instant. The increment lands one deep module at the shell seam, `src/lib/transportBridge.ts` (branch `transport-snapshot`, tickets 01–05): **interface = one snapshot read side** (`subscribe` + `getSnapshot`, read with `useSyncExternalStore`) carrying `{ playing, playheadSec, masterVolume, levels }` — `playheadSec` the canonical Source Mix seconds, `masterVolume` the Master bus value and never a footer-local copy, `levels` the engine's coalesced post-fader L/R sample passed through unmangled — and **exactly four commands: play · pause · seek · setMasterVolume**, the player's fifth verb `seekToStart` riding as `seek(0)` (the shell uses the four verbs only). Publishing is reference-stable: an equal snapshot never publishes and never mints a new object, so React readers never re-render on no-change. Every transport surface — footer disc (`aria-pressed`), fader + % readout, both pip rows, the spine (`currentTime={snapshot.playheadSec}`), the clock chip — reads the ONE snapshot object: same instant by construction, not by vigilance. The 08d channel architecture is untouched by this: the **Stem Player's interface — its props, bridge handoff and all five channels — is byte-unchanged**; its outbound channels land on the module's live adapter (`onTransportControls` → `handoff`, play-state → `setPlaying`, the one position subscription → `setPlayheadSec`, levels → `setLevels`, mixer bridge → `restateMasterVolume`), so ADR-0013's cadence rules — Playhead and phase ride the position channel, levels ride coalesced and are never resampled, the mixer bridge feeds master-volume restatements and nothing per-frame — move from App convention into the module's implementation. The **live-mirror test survives verbatim** (press play, no mixer event, the spine's `aria-valuenow` walks off its start): the one Playhead channel's meaning is unchanged, its bookkeeping just has a home now.

The seam has **two adapters**, which is what makes it a seam and not a hypothetical: the live adapter above, and a **scripted adapter** living in the tests' mock home (`src/__tests__/scriptedTransportAdapter.ts`), injected through the `transport` App prop — it records commands and pushes snapshots, so disc semantics, the % readout, pip classes, spine wiring and clock can all be driven at the App seam with **zero audio machinery**, and the `window.AudioContext` class-swap scaffolds that existed only to fake engine facts retire (the disc's "dispatch play or pause by state" stays UI-side, and the Space fallback is untouched). Seeding and idle rules: with no player wired the snapshot reads `IDLE_TRANSPORT_SNAPSHOT` (not playing, Playhead 0, volume 1.0, silent) and commands are inert no-ops — the pre-separation footer stays sane with no engine anywhere; a restored Session `seed`s `masterVolume` + `playheadSec` from the same mixer payload the Session write-side stores, before the player mounts (the footer's first painted frame is the Session's truth); separation start/completion, new-file decode and the purge all `release()` the handed-off controls and `reset()` to fresh idle defaults. The snapshot is **never a fourth mixer writer** — persistence's single writer stays the mixer bridge and the auto-save cadence is byte-identical. `mm:ss` formatting consolidates into `src/lib/transportClockFormat.ts`: App's clamp-then-floor rule becomes the single rule, App's `formatTransportTime` and StemPlayer's `formatTime` twins retire with their clamp-edge drift, and the Master Waveform's own m:ss ruler face (the wider `aria-valuetext` face) is deliberately untouched — `PLAYHEAD_REPAINT_CEILING_MS = 66` stays byte-stable, no GLOSSARY change (the module takes the glossary's word **Transport**, the concept is unchanged). What is now **banned**: shell code re-mirroring snapshot facts into App component state, and refs mutated during render to carry transport facts — a new shell surface subscribes to the snapshot (one read, not five channels), which is how the DOM contract, the stitch-skin skin and the mixer bridge cadence all rode through this increment byte-identical.
