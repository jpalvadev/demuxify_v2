# Downbeat reading and Beat Grid repair, both user-correctable

A raw comb-tracker output was rejected as the audible truth: its beat positions slip in quiet passages and doubles beats in noisy ones (the Click Track audibly slides), and it says nothing about which beat of a bar is the **Downbeat** — downbeat identity is a listening decision (chord change, bass drop, vocal landing), not a property of beat positions, and even perfect trackers leave it ambiguous. We adopted two defaults in the Tempo Detector worker, each overridable by the user and never presented as proven: an automatic **Downbeat Phase** per Beat Grid — candidate phases 0..Meter−1 scored by low-energy prominence around the beats that land on them, best pick emitted as the grid's Downbeat Phase — with a −/+ one-beat rotation control in the Click Track lane of the Stem Player; and a hybrid snap repair of the Beat Grid against the uniform pulse implied by the chosen tempo — each tracked beat trusted within a tolerance, slips repaired and double-gaps filled, while passages of genuine fluctuation keep their human-feel position. The effective Downbeat Phase persists in the Session next to the Click Track state (a resumed Session has no samples to re-score from); re-locking to a different BPM candidate re-reads the automatic Downbeat against the new grid rather than carrying a nudge tuned against the old one; playback, rail readout, and the Click Track file inside an Export Bundle all obey the same Downbeat Phase; and no new certainty claim is made anywhere — the confidence label stays strictly about the tempo reading, the −/+ affordance itself being what signals adjustability.

Considered options: Downbeat as index-0 assumption (status quo: coin-flip wrong) vs manual-only rotation vs auto-heuristic + manual override (picked); drift as raw tracker positions (glossary-true but slides) vs fully locked uniform grid (kills sliding but flattens real fluctuation and contradicts what Beat Grid is defined _not_ to be) vs hybrid snap (picked); phase persistence ephemeral vs Session sibling (picked); phase reset on re-lock vs carry across grids (picked reset, the old offset is meaningless against halved/doubled positions).

Consequences: the snap tolerance is not a blind number — it is derived from verification runs of the real Source Mix against the `scripts/check_bpm.py` ground-truth anchor and **recorded here as `SNAP_TOLERANCE_SEC = 0.08 s`**: the real beatTrack grid on the 294.1 s reference Source Mix shows all 529 intervals within ±0.014 s of the 107.7 BPM pulse (so repair is a no-op on clean hinted grids), the librosa anchor's own rubato band spans 0.4992–0.6153 s — ±0.058 s around the 0.557 s pulse, genuine fluctuation the repair must keep — and raw-vs-anchor deviation runs p50 0.046 s / p90 0.128 s, the sole divergence window at 210–225 s peaking at 0.30 s of anchor rubato the hinted tracker never sees. 0.08 s therefore sits above the whole natural-fluctuation band while a slip beyond it (the defect probe's 0.31 s slip and its planted skipped pulse) is relocated or filled, keeping human-feel positions untouched; the real-audio seam asserts the guard invariant with the same exported constant. The repaired grid is what every consumer sees (playback, rail, export bytes, export assertions); the Meter's accent pattern itself is unchanged — only which beat is the Downbeat moves. Follow-up to [ADR-0010](./0010-client-side-tempo-detection-and-click-track.md), whose Click-Track-not-a-Stem boundary and ranking-over-certainty honesty this extends to the phase axis.

## Addendum — tolerance replay correction (2026-11-02)

`npm run verify:beat-grid-anchor` (`scripts/verify_beat_grid_anchor.mjs`) replays the tracker half of this derivation on the reference Source Mix: ffmpeg-decoded channel 0 (the `getChannelData(0)` seam the Tempo Detector worker consumes) through the real `beatTrack` @ 107.7, then `repairBeatGrid` transpiled verbatim from `src/lib/beatGridRepair.ts`. Reproduced: 530 beats / 529 intervals, every interval nearest ONE pulse (k = 1 — no double-gaps), repair a structural no-op (530 → 530, nothing inserted or dropped), and the seam guard (worst off-lattice gap after repair 0.0767 s ≤ 0.08 s, the exported constant). Not reproduced: "all 529 intervals within ±0.014 s of the pulse (repair is a no-op)" — 507/529 sit inside that band on any decoder feed or scale; the raw hinted grid carries 22 body slips (max ±0.0350 s) plus a 0.3483 s phase lead-in at beat 0, which repair relocates (beats move up to 0.2088 s). So "no-op" holds structurally, not positionally, on the real mix — consistent with this ADR's premise that raw tracker positions slide. The recorded `SNAP_TOLERANCE_SEC = 0.08 s` survives unchanged: the measured natural band (±0.0350 s body slips; ±0.058 s anchor rubato) sits below it while planted slips (0.31 s) and raw-vs-anchor p90 (0.128 s) sit above. The librosa anchor half still replays via `python scripts/check_bpm.py`.

## Amendment — the lead-in cascade was a defect, fixed (2026-11-02)

The Addendum's "beats move up to 0.2088 s" was measured to be worse than a cosmetic relocation: chaining the lattice off the last EMITTED beat re-measures every later gap against the relocated beat, so the 0.3483 s lead-in (a pickup/anacrusis — musically real, not tracker noise) poisons the whole downstream run. Measured on the reference Source Mix: 25 beats relocated up to 0.2088 s spanning 0.35 s..13.84 s, and against real-hit evidence (librosa strong onsets as the truth) the intro carried 21 clicks >80 ms away from any hit — while the body (20–200 s) sits median 39 ms on the hits.

So `repairBeatGrid` gained a pickup rule (measured defect → red tests → green): a gap off-lattice beyond tolerance no longer relocates unconditionally. If the beat sits ≥ half a pulse after the previous (not a near-duplicate) and the FOLLOWING gap lands on the lattice from the beat itself, the beat vouches for itself: it is kept verbatim (any skipped lattice counts before it are still filled) and the lattice resumes from it. Only an isolated anomaly — the run after it does not vouch for the beat's position — keeps the relocate-onto-chain branch this ADR was tuned for. Contract change: the seam guard "every repaired gap within tolerance of the lattice" becomes "at most the documented lead-in/pickup gaps exceed tolerance"; the amended replay asserts 0 beats relocated, exactly one such gap (the 0.3483 s lead-in, 0.2088 s off lattice), and the defect probe (0.31 s planted slip + planted skipped pulse) still re-inserts. Intro click-vs-hit damage dropped from 18 to 1 in the 0–10 s bin.

What this does NOT fix (measured, deliberately out of scope): the tail (210–294 s) — 54/170 clicks >80 ms from any hit — is bad in the RAW tracker grid too (identical stats), i.e. detection quality, not repair. Any fix there is a new Beat Grid detection seam (detect on the Drums Stem), not this repair.

## Amendment — the Downbeat axis is retired, the Click Track is uniform and loud (2026-11-02)

The user asked for a louder Click Track and, in the same sitting, rejected the accent concept outright: "make all the clicks the same, I don't want to choose where the strong beat is." The phase machinery this ADR adopted — candidate scoring over the analyzed channel, the rail's −/+ pair, the "Beat X of Y · auto" readout, the Session-level `downbeatPhase`, the export's phase parity, and the Click Track's Meter selector (the axis only exists inside a grouping) — was measured as cost, not value: nothing else in the product consumes a bar grouping, and the 92 Hz accent tone was the only thing that made an off-accent click quiet (amp 0.55 vs 1.0), which is what the user heard as "too soft".

So the accent axis is deleted end to end, not defaulted off: `pickDownbeatPhase` and `normalizeDownbeatPhase` are gone from `src/lib/beatGridRepair.ts` (the module keeps only `SNAP_TOLERANCE_SEC` and `repairBeatGrid`), the worker's complete message no longer carries a phase, `synthesizeClickTrackChannel(beatGridSec, sampleRate, durationSec?)` gives every grid position the same tap at amp 1.0 with the tap length raised 0.06 → 0.09 s (peak ≈ 0.97 at 640 Hz, well above the 0.55 the off-accent clicks used to carry, and still inside the Int16 export range — nothing exceeds 1.0 internally, so no export clipping), `ClickTrackMixerState` loses its `meter` field and the rail loses its Meter selector, −/+ buttons and both labels, `App.tsx` drops the `downbeatPhaseRef` sibling, and `sessionStore.ts` stops writing or reading a phase.

No backwards compatibility: old Sessions that wrote a `downbeatPhase` or a `meter` simply do not have those fields anymore, and nothing re-guesses them — a resumed Session gets the lane exactly as stored now. The `Meter`, `Downbeat` and `Downbeat Phase` GLOSSARY entries are deleted (the vocabulary no longer exists in the product), and the retired affordances are pinned by absence assertions at the rail seam (`click-track-meter-label`, `click-track-meter-selector`, `click-track-phase-control`, `click-track-phase-minus`/`plus` all null, no button phrased around a Downbeat) and at the engine seam (peaks equal at every grid position, peak > 0.9).

What survives of this ADR is the other half of its title: the Beat Grid hybrid snap repair, its recorded `SNAP_TOLERANCE_SEC = 0.08 s` derivation, the pickup-rule amendment, and the seam guard replay — all untouched by this retirement, since the repair argues about beat POSITIONS, which is the one thing that was always measurable.
